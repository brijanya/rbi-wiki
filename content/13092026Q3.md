### Capital Markets & Microstructure (10 Marks): What is the Closing Auction Session (CAS) in equity markets? Explain how SEBI’s proposed integration of a Blended Volume-Weighted Average Price (VWAP) aims to curb artificial volatility and price manipulation during expiry-day derivatives settlements.

Market microstructure interventions by regulators are essential for maintaining price discovery efficiency and investor protection. In response to extreme market volatility observed after the rollout of the **Closing Auction Session (CAS)** on August 3, 2026, the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) issued a consultation paper proposing sweeping reforms to the expiry-day settlement of derivatives.

#### 1. What is the Closing Auction Session (CAS)?
The Closing Auction Session (CAS) is a specialized trading window at the end of the trading day designed to discover a single, efficient closing price for securities.
1. **Mechanism:** Unlike the Continuous Trading Session (CTS) where buy and sell orders are matched immediately, the CAS collects all orders over a specific timeframe (e.g., 10 minutes). The exchange then uses an algorithm to calculate an **Indicative Equilibrium Price (IEP)**—the price at which the maximum volume of shares can be traded. All eligible trades are executed simultaneously at this single price.
2. **Original Objective:** CAS was introduced to minimize the market impact of large institutional orders (such as index fund rebalancing) placed near the close, thereby preventing erratic price swings in the final minutes of continuous trading.

#### 2. The Core Issue: Expiry-Day Volatility and Manipulation
While CAS is efficient for cash markets, its linkage to the Futures and Options (F&O) segment created severe anomalies on expiry days.
1. **The Disconnect:** Derivatives trading continued alongside the CAS. A drop in cash market volumes during the auction meant that relatively small orders in the CAS could heavily skew the closing price of the underlying asset.
2. **Artificial Volatility:** Options traders complained of sharp, unpredictable losses because the settlement price of their expiring contracts was being dictated by the thin liquidity and evolving IEPs of the CAS.
3. **Spoofing:** Market participants were placing large orders in the CAS to manipulate the IEP and then abruptly cancelling them before execution, artificially inflating or deflating index values.

#### 3. SEBI’s Proposed "Blended VWAP" Framework
To curb these structural vulnerabilities, SEBI has proposed moving the derivatives settlement price calculation to a **Blended Volume-Weighted Average Price (VWAP)**.
1. **The Blend:** The settlement price will be calculated using trades executed during the last 30 minutes of the Continuous Trading Session (CTS) combined with the trades executed during the 10-minute CAS.
2. **Dynamic Weightage:** Instead of assigning a fixed 50-50 weight to both sessions, the contribution of the CTS and the CAS to the final price will be determined dynamically by the actual traded value in each respective window.
SEBI has also proposed an interim alternative to temporarily revert to the old CTS VWAP method for at least one year before transitioning to the Blended VWAP.

#### 4. How the Blended VWAP and Allied Reforms Curb Manipulation
The proposed framework targets manipulation through a multi-pronged approach:
1. **Diluting Rogue Orders:** By expanding the time horizon to include 30 minutes of continuous trading, the Blended VWAP vastly broadens the volume base. It becomes mathematically and financially prohibitive for manipulators to skew the settlement price using a few last-minute orders in the CAS.
2. **Halting Indicative Index Value (IIV) Broadcasts:** SEBI proposed discontinuing the dissemination of the IIV during the CAS. Because the IEP is an evolving metric, broadcasting an estimated index value caused panic and speculative frenzy among options traders. Stopping this prevents algorithmic and manual trading from front-running false index signals.
3. **Restricting Order Cancellations:** To prevent spoofing, limit orders placed between 1% and 3% away from the reference price cannot be cancelled during the CAS (though they can be modified to improve the price). This forces participants to commit to their trades, reducing "fake" demand or supply.
4. **Reducing Post-CAS Derivatives Window:** SEBI proposed cutting the derivatives trading window from 10 minutes to 5 minutes post-CAS. This minimizes the timeframe for speculative bets after the price discovery of the underlying asset is already complete.

SEBI’s Blended VWAP proposal is a structural calibration that seeks to align the cash market's price discovery process with the F&O market's settlement needs. By anchoring the settlement price to a broader volume base and restricting manipulative order-book tactics, the regulator aims to restore stability, enhance market integrity, and protect options traders from synthetic expiry-day volatility.
