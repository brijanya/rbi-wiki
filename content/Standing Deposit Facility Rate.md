The standing deposit facility (SDF) is a liquidity management tool used by the RBI to absorb surplus cash from commercial banks without needing to provide any government security as collateral. When banks have excess cash which is not being lent out, they park it with the RBI at the SDF rate to earn interest. 
Before the SDF, RBI used the reverse repo to absorb excess liquidity from the banking system. However this required the RBI to hand over the government securities as collateral to the banks. In case where the liquidity was far more than the government securities RBI could pledge, the RBI would not be able to absorb the excess cash. To remove this, SDF was introduced.
### Historical Use
The SDF was added to the RBI act in 2018 however, the actual use of the facility was made to soak up the trillions of Rupees of excess liquidity in the system post pandemic in 2022.

By introducing SDF rate at 3.75%, RBI absorbed the excess liquidity without loosing its government security reserves.

SDF along with the repo rate and the marginal standing facility (MSF) forms the Liquidity Adjustment Facility Corridor.
